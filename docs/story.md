# The story

The narrative behind The Gilded Griffin: who Mirabel is, what went wrong, why she asked EPAM for help, and how that fiction connects to the real purpose of this repository. It is the source material for the leadership session's trailer video and for anything else that needs to tell the story (slides, an introduction, a feature request written in character).

What follows from these:

- **It is a teaser, so it must stand on its own.** The audience may see it hours or days before the session. It cannot assume they know the shop, Mirabel or what a hyperfactory is, and it must leave them knowing what they will do when they arrive. It says "soon", not "today".
- **A narrator** can speak about Mirabel with affection and can step outside the fiction for the real point. Mirabel's own voice is saved for the one moment that matters most: her request.
- **The bounty board is the hook.** The monster trouble is the problem the trailer dwells on, and the request is asked for, never shown built. The film names the questions the room will settle without answering them.
- **The ending has two parts**, matching the session: "first, together, the bounty board", then "after that, her list is yours".

## The story in one paragraph

Mirabel Thistlewick runs a much-loved bookshop in a town full of elves, dwarves, orcs and the occasional dragon. The shop has outgrown her paper ledger: she does not know what is in stock, who her best patrons are, or what the monsters are costing her. She cannot afford a guild of scribes working for a year. So she engages EPAM, who bring something new: a team of people directing a factory of tireless AI agents that can turn her requests into working, tested software in hours instead of seasons. Today, the leaders in the room will run that factory themselves.

## The two layers

The trailer has to hold both of these at once.

| The fiction | What it stands for |

| Her growing list of wants | A product backlog |
| EPAM, engaged to build her system | EPAM Canada, engaged by clients to do the same |
| The Scribes' Guild and its quotation: a dozen scribes, a year and a day, and a price that changes with every request | A competitor bringing a traditional approach to a bid |
| Why Mirabel sent for EPAM instead: a small team and a scriptorium | What we bring to a bid that a traditional approach cannot |
| The factory of agents | An SDLC hyperfactory: agents that take a feature request through elaboration, planning, development, testing and deployment |
| The people directing the factory | The directors and above in the room, from delivery and account management |

**The message to land.** The client's need is ordinary and familiar. What has changed is how we meet it. Leaders do not need to be engineers to direct this way of working, and after today they will have done it.

## Mirabel

Established in the codebase and personas:

- Proprietor of The Gilded Griffin, Books & Curios, at No. 7 Lantern Street, Emberhollow. "Third lantern past the fountain." The building occasionally moves.
- A halfling in her fifties who inherited the shop from her aunt and has run it for twenty years.
- Author of *The Bookwyrm and Other Shelf Pests*, "written from bitter experience".
- Does the books at the kitchen table after closing, with tea.
- Warm, dry, practical. She gave tea and a book to a knight who had just been bitten by her merchandise.
- Her line: *"I know every book on these shelves. What I do not know is whether we made any money this month."*

## The world

- **The shop.** Established "in the Year of the Quiet Comet". Sign of the winged book. A fireproof reading nook, a locked cabinet of restricted tomes, a cellar nobody opens, and a shop cat in the window. The cash box is a reformed mimic.
- **The shop's promise.** *"Stories for every folk. Shelter from every storm."*
- **The folk.** Humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, gnomes, orcs, goblins, dragonborn and fae all shop here. Tall shelves have ladders; low shelves have cushions.
- **The staff.** Mirabel, and Pip Underbough, a young and eager clerk who is usually alone at the counter when things go wrong.
- **The monsters.** Pests, not villains. Bookwyrms eat stock, mimics pose as first editions, poltergeists reshelve by colour, ink imps add rude footnotes. The shop is "occasionally beset, never closed". Four wards protect it; one is flickering.
- **Currency.** Gold, silver and copper pieces.

### The trouble the trailer teases: the bounty board

When something dire gets into the shop, Mirabel sends Pip running to the tavern to find adventurers. The price is agreed in a doorway and remembered differently by each side. Nothing records who did the job or what it cost.

- Last week a mimic posing as a signed first edition bit Sir Aldric. Adventurers were hired for 3 gold pieces. The mimic now works as the shop's cash box. Because that story ended happily, the trailer tells it early as a charming one and uses a different incident for the bounty:
- In the trailer, the seal on the cellar door (the ward that has long been flickering) goes out, and a dust wraith comes up the stairs. It harms nobody, but it frightens the queue and makes everyone sneeze, and it is plainly a job for adventurers. Pip runs to the tavern. The demo data has a small dust wraith on the top shelves already; the trailer's is its dire cousin.
- Bran Holloway, who leads the party that took the job, says: *"Three gold for a mimic is robbery. It had teeth on the inside."*
- Mirabel's request, as written in the backlog: *"For a dire incident I want to post a bounty for adventurers."* Add a bounty in gold to an incident, record who claimed it, and include bounties paid in the owner's figures.

It makes a good tease because everyone understands it at once, the result is visible on screen, and it has rules worth working out: who may post a bounty, for which incidents, who may claim one, and what happens to the takings.

## Why she engaged EPAM (proposed)

This part of the story did not exist before this document. A suggested version:

- **What she tried first.** A paper ledger, then her nephew's enchanted abacus, then a quote from the Scribes' Guild: a dozen scribes, a year and a day, and more gold than the shop takes in a decade. Everything she asks for changes the price.
- **What she needs.** Not one grand system delivered in a year. A partner who can take a request on Moonday and show her something working by Fireday, and who will still be there for the next request, and the next.
- **Why EPAM.** They understood the shop before they talked about the system. And they did not bring a dozen scribes. They brought a small team and a factory: tireless agents that elaborate the request, plan the work, build it, test it and put it on the shelf, with people she trusts deciding what gets built and checking that it is right.
- **What she got first.** The system as it stands today: a shop front for the public, a counter for her and Pip, the catalog, the guest book and the incident board. It works. Now she has a list.

**Confirmed:** EPAM appears by name inside the fiction. The Scribes' Guild quotation is confirmed too, and matters: it is the competitor's traditional bid, and Mirabel chose EPAM because of what EPAM brought instead. The trailer adds one line to make the point: "Ask on Moonday; see it working by Fireday."

### The factory as a metaphor (proposed)

If the factory is shown, it is shown in the world's own terms, never as software screens. One suggestion, in keeping with a bookshop:

- **A scriptorium that never sleeps.** A workshop of enchanted quills, each with one job. A request arrives as a note in Mirabel's hand, and passes from desk to desk:
  - one quill asks the questions she had not thought of (elaboration);
  - one draws up the plan (planning);
  - several write at once (development);
  - one reads every page back, looking for mistakes (testing);
  - one carries the finished work to the shop and puts it on the shelf (deployment).
- **People from EPAM walk the floor.** They decide what is written, read what the quills produce, and send a page back when it is not right. The quills are tireless; the judgement is human.

This keeps the five stages visible and in order, which is the thing the audience most needs to take away, and it stays honest that people direct and check the work.

## Where the story stands (proposed)

The current codebase is **the end of chapter one**: EPAM has delivered the first working version. The trailer sets up **chapter two**, which the room writes during the session: Mirabel has more requests, and the leaders run the factory that delivers them.

This framing matters because it makes the existing system part of the story rather than a contradiction of it, and makes the audience the heroes of the next act.

## The shape of the trailer

As built in the second cut.

1. **The shop.** Night, lanterns, the glowing window. Then inside: every folk, ladders for the tall shelves, cushions for the low ones.
2. **The pests.** Mirabel's own field guide: bookwyrm, poltergeist, ink imp. The mimic, the bitten knight, five stars, and the cash box.
3. **Mirabel.** At the kitchen table with tea and a ledger that does not add up.
4. **The trouble.** What the ledger cannot tell her about her stock, her patrons and her monsters. Then the dire one: the cellar seal goes out, a dust wraith rises, and Pip sprints to the tavern to haggle with adventurers. Nobody writes it down.
5. **The traditional answer.** The Scribes' Guild quotation: a dozen scribes, a year and a day, and every change alters the price.
6. **The turn, and the factory.** The Guild's answer and EPAM's, side by side. Then the scriptorium (see [above](#the-factory-as-a-metaphor-proposed)): a request goes in; questions, a plan, the work, the checking and the delivery come out. Fast, visible, checked by people.
7. **Chapter one delivered.** The real system on screen: the landing page, the counter, the incident board.
8. **The request.** Mirabel's list, and at the top of it, in her own voice: *"For a dire incident I want to post a bounty for adventurers."* The narrator asks who may post one, who may claim one, and what happens if two parties do.
9. **Chapter two.** The narrator turns to the audience: soon, you run the scriptorium. First the bounty board together, through all five stages; then the rest of the list is yours.

## Tone

- **Light whimsy, played straight.** The humour is dry and affectionate. Nobody winks at the camera. Mirabel takes her bookwyrm problem as seriously as any client takes theirs.
- **Cozy, never grim.** Monsters are a nuisance, not a threat. Nobody is hurt. Sir Aldric was bitten and gave five stars.
- **Respectful of the client.** Mirabel is sharp and capable. She is short of time and tools, not sense.
- **Honest about the factory.** Agents do the work; people direct and check it. Avoid implying that no people are involved or that it is magic. In this world of all places, it is the one thing that is not.
- **Plain language** when the trailer explains the real point. The audience has mixed backgrounds.
